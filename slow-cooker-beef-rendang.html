Slow Cooker Beef Rendang

Tender beef in a rich coconut, lemongrass and ginger sauce with toasted coconut. Pan prep at the start, then finish entirely in the slow cooker.

Time to allow: 8 hr 5 min–9 hr 50 min totalIncludes the final high-heat stage.

Serves 2 generously as written. Move the slider to scale the ingredients. Break whole spices into pieces when making a smaller batch. Cooking times are guides; use a slow cooker size suited to your batch and check the beef for tenderness.

Method

  1. 1

    Before starting the beef, stir the desiccated coconut in a dry frying pan over a medium-low heat until deep golden. Transfer immediately to a bowl and set aside. This is done during prep, so there is no pan work at the end.

  2. 2

    Blend the spice paste ingredients until fairly smooth, adding just enough water to help the blender work. Keep the paste thick rather than runny.

  3. 3

    Heat the oil in the frying pan. Brown the beef in batches, then transfer to the slow cooker.

  4. 4

    Lower the heat and fry the paste in the same pan for 4–5 minutes, stirring, until fragrant. Add a splash of the measured coconut milk to loosen any browned bits, then scrape everything into the slow cooker.

  5. 5

    Add the remaining coconut milk, lime leaves, cinnamon, star anise, tamarind, sugar and salt. Stir, cover and cook on low for 7–8 hours, until the beef yields easily to a fork. Do not add stock or extra water.

  6. 6

    Remove the cinnamon, star anise and lime leaves. Gently stir in the toasted coconut, switch to high and remove the lid. Cook uncovered for about 45–90 minutes, stirring gently every 15–20 minutes, until the sauce is as thick as you like. The time depends on your cooker and batch size; the sauce should remain hot and gently bubbling. There is no need to transfer it to a saucepan.

  7. 7

    Taste and adjust the salt and tamarind, then serve straight from the slow cooker. This version has a rich, thick sauce but will usually be saucier and less caramelised than rendang finished in a pan.

Tips & leftovers

Storage & leftovers

Divide into shallow containers to cool, refrigerate within 2 hours and eat within 48 hours.

Freezing

Freeze cooled portions with their sauce for up to 2 months for best quality. Label before freezing. Keep the toasted coconut mixed into the sauce; coconut milk may separate slightly.

Reheating

Reheat once on the hob or in the microwave until steaming hot throughout; stir and turn meat pieces so the centre heats evenly. Do not use the slow cooker to reheat chilled leftovers. Stir gently to avoid breaking the tender beef; add water sparingly to keep the thick sauce.

Substitutions

Use beef chuck or braising steak as written; lean quick-fry steak dries out during long cooking. Lemon zest changes the flavour but can replace unavailable lime leaves; remove whole spices before serving.

Keeping leftovers safe

Cool cooked leftovers in small portions and refrigerate within 2 hours. Keep the fridge at 0–5°C and the freezer around −18°C. Freeze suitable leftovers promptly. Defrost fully in the fridge and use within 24 hours. Reheat only once, until steaming hot all the way through. Follow any shorter use-by or opened-pack instructions; using older cooked ingredients does not restart their storage time.

Food-safety guidance: Food Standards Agency: chilling and freezing · reheating leftovers.
