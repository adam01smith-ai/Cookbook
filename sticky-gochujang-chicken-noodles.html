Sticky Gochujang Chicken Noodles

Method

  1. 1

    Mix the sauce ingredients together, keeping the lime juice aside.

  2. 2

    Heat the oil in a large frying pan over a medium-high heat. Add the chicken and fry for 6–8 minutes, stirring occasionally, until cooked through.

  3. 3

    Add the pepper and the white parts of the pak choi. Stir-fry for 3 minutes.

  4. 4

    Add the garlic and ginger and cook for 30 seconds.

  5. 5

    Pour in the sauce and simmer for 2 minutes.

  6. 6

    Prepare the noodles according to their packet instructions, then add them to the pan with the pak choi leaves. Toss until coated and the leaves have wilted.

  7. 7

    Stir in the lime juice. Divide between bowls and scatter over the spring onions and sesame seeds.

Tips & leftovers

Storage & leftovers

Cool promptly in shallow portions, refrigerate covered within 2 hours and eat within 48 hours.

Freezing

Freeze cooled portions for up to 2 months for best quality, but noodles and vegetables will soften. Keep fresh garnishes separate. Add sesame seeds, lime juice and spring onion garnish after reheating.

Reheating

Reheat once in a frying pan with a splash of water, tossing until steaming hot throughout. In the microwave, stir or turn halfway and check the centre is hot. Use enough water to loosen the sticky glaze without turning it into soup.

Substitutions

Use chicken breast as written. Thinly sliced cabbage can replace pak choi; add it before tender leaves. Egg noodles can replace udon if cooked to their packet instructions.

Keeping leftovers safe

Cool cooked leftovers in small portions and refrigerate within 2 hours. Keep the fridge at 0–5°C and the freezer around −18°C. Freeze suitable leftovers promptly. Defrost fully in the fridge and use within 24 hours. Reheat only once, until steaming hot all the way through. Follow any shorter use-by or opened-pack instructions; using older cooked ingredients does not restart their storage time.

Food-safety guidance: Food Standards Agency: chilling and freezing · reheating leftovers.
