French Onion Soup

Golden caramelised onions in a rich beef and white wine broth, topped with baguette and melted Gruyère. Defaults to 2 servings; the full recipe serves 4.

Time to allow: 1 hr 5 min–1 hr 10 min total

Prep15 mins
Cook50–55 mins

Method

  1. 1

    Soften the onions

    Melt the butter with the olive oil in a large, heavy-based pan. Add the onions, cover and fry gently for 10 minutes until soft.
  2. 2

    Caramelise

    Sprinkle in the sugar and cook for about 20 minutes more, stirring frequently, until the onions are deeply golden, full of flavour and very soft. Take care towards the end so they do not burn. If they need longer to caramelise, give them more time over a gentle heat.
  3. 3

    Add garlic and flour

    Add the sliced garlic for the final few minutes of the onions’ cooking time. Sprinkle in the plain flour and stir well.
  4. 4

    Add wine and stock

    Increase the heat and keep stirring as you gradually add the dry white wine, followed by the hot beef stock. Cover and simmer for 15–20 minutes.
  5. 5

    Toast the baguette

    Heat the grill and toast the baguette slices. Ladle the soup into bowls that are suitable for use under the grill.
  6. 6

    Melt the cheese

    Put one or two pieces of toast on each bowl of soup and pile on the grated Gruyère. Grill until melted and golden. Alternatively, melt the cheese on the toasts separately under the grill, then add them to the soup to serve.

Tips & leftovers

Storage & leftovers

Cool in shallow containers, refrigerate covered within 2 hours and eat within 48 hours. Keep baguette and cheese separate from the soup until serving.

Freezing

Freeze cooled portions in sealed, labelled containers for up to 2 months for best quality. Leave expansion space. Freeze the onion soup without the bread and cheese topping.

Reheating

Reheat once in a saucepan, stirring and adding a splash of water or stock, until steaming hot throughout. Heat the soup fully first, then add fresh toast and cheese and grill until melted.

Substitutions

Use Emmental or mature cheddar instead of Gruyère. Replace wine with extra stock and a little lemon juice to taste. Vegetable stock and vegetarian cheese give a meat-free variation.

Keeping leftovers safe

Cool cooked leftovers in small portions and refrigerate within 2 hours. Keep the fridge at 0–5°C and the freezer around −18°C. Freeze suitable leftovers promptly. Defrost fully in the fridge and use within 24 hours. Reheat only once, until steaming hot all the way through. Follow any shorter use-by or opened-pack instructions; using older cooked ingredients does not restart their storage time.
