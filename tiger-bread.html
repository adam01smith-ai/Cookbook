Method

  1. 1

    Mix, knead and prove

    Tip the flour into a large mixing bowl. Stir through the yeast, sugar and salt. Make a well in the middle and gradually pour in the water. Swiftly mix together, then turn out onto a lightly floured surface. Knead for 8–10 minutes or until smooth and elastic. Lightly oil a large mixing bowl, put the dough in the bowl, cover and leave in a warm place for 1 hour or until doubled in size.
  2. 2

    Shape and prove again

    Once risen, tip the dough onto a worktop and knead it three times. Shape into an oval. If making multiple loaves, divide the dough into equal portions first and shape each into an oval. Lightly flour a baking sheet and sit the loaf on it, using additional sheets as needed for multiple loaves. Cover loosely with lightly oiled cling film and leave for another 45 minutes–1 hour or until doubled in size again.
  3. 3

    Add the topping and bake

    Heat the oven to 200°C / 180°C fan / gas 6. Whisk all the topping ingredients together in a small bowl to make a spreadable paste, adding more water and/or rice flour if necessary. Rest for 5 minutes, then gently spread over the loaf with a palette knife. Put the baking sheet in the centre of the oven and bake for 35 minutes. The cooked loaf should sound hollow when tapped underneath and feel light for its size. Leave to cool completely before slicing.

Tips & leftovers

Storage & leftovers

Let the loaf cool completely, then store wrapped in a clean bread bag or bread bin in a cool, dry place. Eat within 2 days for best texture; refrigeration makes plain bread stale faster. Discard the whole loaf if mould appears.

Freezing

Slice cooled bread, wrap well and freeze for up to 2 months for best quality. Separate slices so you can take out only what you need. Thaw covered at room temperature, or toast individual slices from frozen.

Reheating

Toast slices, or refresh a loaf briefly in an oven at 180°C / 160°C fan. Heat only what you need so repeated warming does not dry it out.

Substitutions

Keep rice flour in the topping for its characteristic crackle; wheat flour gives a different crust. Neutral oil can replace sesame oil, with a milder flavour.

Keeping leftovers safe

These notes are for the baked, unfilled loaf. Keep bread dry and well wrapped; discard the whole loaf if mould appears. Once you add perishable fillings or toppings, follow their fridge storage guidance instead. Keep the freezer around −18°C and take out only the slices you need.

Food-safety guidance: Food Standards Agency: chilling and freezing · reheating leftovers.
