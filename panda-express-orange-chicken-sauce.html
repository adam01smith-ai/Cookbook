Method

  1. 1
    Heat a pan over a medium heat and add the oil.
  2. 2
    Once the oil begins to shimmer, add the chilli flakes, ginger and garlic. Cook for 30 seconds, stirring constantly.
  3. 3
    Add the sugar and brown sugar, and stir to combine.
  4. 4
    Add the orange juice and allow the sugars to begin to dissolve, stirring occasionally.
  5. 5
    Add the vinegar and soy sauce, and stir to combine.
  6. 6
    Whisk the cornflour and water together in a separate bowl until smooth. Add to the pan and stir.
  7. 7
    Continue to cook the sauce until it reaches a maple syrup consistency.
  8. 8
    Add the fried chicken and stir until completely coated in the sauce.
  9. 9
    Top with sesame oil.

This recipe is for the sauce. Prepare your fried chicken separately.

Tips & leftovers

Storage & leftovers

Cool promptly and refrigerate in a clean covered container within 2 hours. Use within 48 hours; this homemade sauce is not shelf-stable.

Freezing

Freeze cooled sauce in small labelled portions for up to 2 months for best quality. Leave room for expansion. The cornflour-thickened sauce can loosen after thawing; stir it well.

Reheating

Reheat only the amount needed, once, in a saucepan, stirring until steaming hot throughout. Add a little water if too thick. If still too thin after heating, simmer briefly while stirring.

Substitutions

Use tamari instead of soy sauce if needed, checking labels. Fresh orange juice works; add chilli gradually. The cornflour is the thickener, so do not swap it spoon-for-spoon with ordinary flour.

Keeping leftovers safe

Cool cooked leftovers in small portions and refrigerate within 2 hours. Keep the fridge at 0–5°C and the freezer around −18°C. Freeze suitable leftovers promptly. Defrost fully in the fridge and use within 24 hours. Reheat only once, until steaming hot all the way through. Follow any shorter use-by or opened-pack instructions; using older cooked ingredients does not restart their storage time.

Food-safety guidance: Food Standards Agency: chilling and freezing · reheating leftovers.
